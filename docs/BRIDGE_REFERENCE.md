# Sydney Harbour Bridge refinement

Reference review: 10 September 2026. Geometry is original procedural work. The linked photographs were inspected as visual references; no third-party photograph, texture or downloaded 3D model is included in the game.

- [Australian Heritage Database: Sydney Harbour Bridge](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/sydney-harbour-bridge.pdf): independent official description of the twin 28-panel trusses, 30m separation and 89m pylons.

- [Full-span photograph, 16 October 2025](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sydney_Harbour_Bridge-16_October_2025.jpg): inspected at full browser viewport for the changing truss depth, broad flat pylon crowns, end bearings and lower horizontal deck ribbon.
- [Road-level photograph from a car](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:View_from_car_sydney_harbour_bridge.JPG): inspected at full browser viewport for the overhead bracing, vertical hangers, western railway and clear carriageway.
- [Destination NSW: Pylon Museum and Lookout](https://www.sydney.com/in/destinations/sydney/sydney-city/the-rocks/attractions/sydney-harbour-bridge-pylon-museum-and-lookout): its portrait photograph was inspected for grey granite, tapered sides, a principal round-headed opening, balcony, slender slit windows, dressed corner strips and restrained flat crown. ## Model decisions

| Element | Implemented geometry |
| --- | --- |
| Main span and datum | Mapped pylon axis corrected on 11 September; 503m arch span, 49m deck, deck top 54m above game water |
| Arch planes | Two 28-panel trusses, 30m between their centre planes |
| Arch section | 57m deep beside the pylons, 18m at the crown; top steel surface approximately 134m |
| Structural detail | Upper and lower chords, alternating diagonals, vertical members, hangers, flange strips, splice plates and rivet heads |
| Traffic | Six lanes between the arches and two eastern lanes, western double railway, outer walking/cycle paths |
| Pylons | Four tapered grey granite-faced masses terminating near 89m, with arched recesses, balconies, slit windows, stone trim and flat lookout parapets |
| Approaches | 49m wide south game ramp and mapped curved Bradfield north approach, estimated grade, low approach trusses, piers and continuous raised footways |

This is a playable reference-based reconstruction, not a survey-grade model. The arch curves are analytic approximations constrained by the official major dimensions. Exact rivet positions, every stone course, railway operations, signage and the complete real approach road network are simplified. The outer walking/cycle surfaces are 2.2m wide in this model. The 11 September [geographic audit](GEOGRAPHIC_ALIGNMENT.md) corrects the arch midpoint and four pylon positions against OSM; the former endpoints were not surveyed. The south ground connection moves 2.26m onto that axis. The north approach is now a real mapped 538.72m Bradfield Highway curve rather than the former straight ramp displaced to the east; its grade and 49m-wide allocation remain estimates. See the geographic audit for source-way chains, connection coordinates and remaining elevation/interchange limits.

The former steel across the roadway came from interpolating lateral X-braces through the deck elevation near both arch ends. Cross-bracing now has an explicit minimum-clearance guard. Other structural members stay in their side planes or below the deck. The old maintenance steps across the walking line were removed. Raised paths now have matching physical colliders.

The old 35m approaches did not align with the 49m span. Both approaches now match the full width. Main span and south support share an axis; the curved northern support follows the real centreline and joins the span with a matching first cross-section. This removes the north-end wedge-shaped collision cracks found by dense testing. The southern ground connector first follows the ramp's outward direction, then joins the street without cutting through a divider. World generation separately reserves the complete bridge and approach corridor against buildings and ground clutter.

## Integration and validation

`game/scripts/bridge_landmark.gd` exposes `build(world)`, `pos(station, height, across)`, `ramp_position(label, fraction)`, `ramp_basis(label, fraction)`, `ROAD_LANES` and `WALK_X`. The existing 42 main-deck and 28-per-side ramp damage IDs are retained; revised pylon portal/detail pieces do not imply every former decorative ID is unchanged. One welded continuous concave road skin and two continuous footway skins span all three regions on the same StaticBody3D. North matches sixteen subdivisions per saved deck component; there are no transverse end faces inside an intact run. Jolt internal-edge handling replaces the false collision normals encountered with ordinary Godot Physics. Separate primitive boxes also produced false entry-edge contacts at sloped/flat joins under Jolt, so they are no longer used as bridge support. The smooth grade and averaged cross-section frames avoid the coarse mesh folds/edge contacts found in earlier prototypes. Curved guardrails and visible road finishes use the same sections. Damaged components split the continuous skins into shorter intact runs, preserving actual collision holes. The world queues a support refresh after damage, save loading and repair. Decorative parts attach to their removable component and are merged by the existing spatial structure batching system.

Run from the repository root:

```sh
./tools/runtime/godot --headless --path game --script ../source/bridge_landmark_test.gd
./tools/runtime/godot --path game --script ../source/bridge_landmark_test.gd -- --capture
```

The test checks actual physics colliders throughout all eight road lanes and both outer paths on the main span and both approaches. It probes a 2.4m wide, 5.5m high vehicle clearance volume and a walking/camera volume, samples both span joins every 5cm, and checks all ground exit legs. Captures use the native Metal Forward+ renderer and are written under `reports/bridge-refinement/`. The suite also destroys a deck, reloads saved deck/approach damage and repairs the bridge, checking actual collision holes and restored supports. The formal Godot 4.7.2 stable / Jolt Physics run passes 29 assertions and 11,345 shape probes across the entire mapped curve, with `HARBOR_WORLD_READY` and zero script errors; [full production geometry log](evidence/final-bridge-v012.txt). Passing these geometric checks does not replace a real driving test; vehicle traversal is tested separately by the full-scene integration suite.

The historical 0.1.1 straight-route inputs-only driving run recorded in `reports/bridge-refinement/drive-thin-top.log` completed both directions in 82.43 / 82.45 seconds. South to north retained 100 health; north to south retained **99.887542** health after one small physics edge contact at the north ramp/main-span crest. This residual 0.112458 loss is recorded honestly; it is not a both-directions-100 result. That older support revision reduced the former repeated large pitch impulses and damage but retained the stated residual contact; the later continuous skin and Jolt validation below supersede it. Vehicle impact thresholds were not raised to hide geometry contacts.


11 September geographic revision: the **formal production Godot 4.7.2 stable / Jolt Physics scene** completes the corrected bridge and mapped curved north approach using ordinary driving inputs in **85.8167s south→north / 85.5333s north→south**, with **100 health both ways, zero impacts**, and maximum lateral route errors 0.4054/0.3399m. This exactly reproduces the isolated Jolt comparison and supersedes the older straight-route result above. The production run contains `HARBOR_WORLD_READY`, zero script errors and exits successfully; [driving result](evidence/bridge-drive-v012.json), [full production log](evidence/final-bridge-drive-v012.txt). The controlled initial vehicle clearance is 1.0m; the old 1.3m test placement artificially dropped the car hard enough to record a landing at the new near-horizontal north end. The driving damage formula is unchanged. This formal run also starts the actual two-shore `race` job on its first outward drive: eight production checkpoints advance naturally through normal main-scene physics context, with the final gate reached at the actual job elapsed time of 84.85s, completion count +1, and money/lifetime earnings both **+$2,232 ($2,000 base and the full $232 timing bonus)**. The test reads the completed job dictionary and confirms zero race incidents and credited bonus equal to `int((240−completion_elapsed)×1.5)`; it permits $1 rounding tolerance, but this run matches exactly. The second drive confirms no repeated completion or payment. The test never moves the vehicle to gates or calls the task-context updater directly. Incidents from other vehicles no longer reduce the current task reward; they retain ordinary world damage and NPC reactions.

The [official Jolt documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/using_jolt_physics.html#ghost-collisions) explains active triangle-edge detection and enhanced internal-edge removal, including the limitation at different bodies. The final production configuration enables Jolt and enhanced removal for simulation/queries/motion queries, and raises `physics/jolt_physics_3d/limits/max_bodies` to 65536 because the city's roughly 21,000 structural bodies exceed the default 10240. [The official setting reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_projectsettings.html#class-projectsettings-property-physics-jolt-physics-3d-limits-max-bodies) documents that limit. The final project has adopted this configuration. Remaining vehicle/flight compatibility and exported-app native visual review are checked separately; a bridge-only success is not a claim that every physics interaction or city street has been validated.
