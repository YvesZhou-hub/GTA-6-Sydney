## Westpac

[Westpac's current location page](https://www.westpac.com.au/about-westpac/global-locations/westpac-australia/) identifies 275 Kent Street as its head office. [The architect JPW's project account](https://jpw.com.au/projects/westpac-place/) describes two offset towers around a central core, metal/glass facade blades, 32 campus floors and a raised commercial mass above public ground. [CTBUH's building record](https://www.skyscrapercenter.com/building/westpac-place/2995) gives 166m architectural height and its own 35-floor count; this counting difference is not presented as an exact tenancy floor plan.

Actual photographs viewed in the browser: [JPW upper tower view](https://jpw.com.au/site/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/JPW_WestpacPlace_001.jpeg), [JPW public-ground/lobby view](https://jpw.com.au/site/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/JPW_WestpacPlace_002-604x1200.jpeg), and the public lawn/terrace photo on the same project page. They show the blue glazed vertical planes, different horizontal shading rhythms of the two tower wings, slender corner blades, and the raised glass volume over a generous public forecourt. These features drive the separate tower geometry; the entire site's outline is not extruded to the roof.

The small mapped roof elements are reconstructed as the characteristic pale beacon and adjacent dark plant, within the documented overall 166m envelope. Red bars express the recognizable architectural feature; they do not display live pressure data. [The lighting contractor's refurbishment account](https://www.afterglowcontrols.com/post/reimagining-a-city-icon-westpac-building-fa%C3%A7ade-lighting-refurbishment) corroborates the architectural lighting feature and its recent work. The model has no claimed real-time weather function.

[Network Architectural's current cladding project](https://www.networkarchitectural.com.au/projects/275-kent-street-sydney-in-progress/) describes replacement panels in dark/metallic grays. Its search-indexed direct photograph URL returned 404 during live viewing, so it was not treated as a visually inspected image. The available architect photographs support the massing; every current cladding panel is not asserted to be an as-built match. No Westpac logo is fabricated in a different location.

## Commonwealth Bank Place

[CBA investor relations](https://www.commbank.com.au/about-us/investors/enquiries.html) lists Commonwealth Bank Place South, Level1, 11 Harbour Street. The bank's [head-office history](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/newsroom/2020/11/commonwealth-bank-head-offices.html) labels Darling Park's period as 2010–2022 and describes the transition to the refurbished paired Commonwealth Bank Place buildings. The older 201 Sussex Street offices, Martin Place heritage banks and South Eveleigh campus must not be substituted for this current South-building address.

[FJC Studio's Darling Quarter account](https://fjcstudio.com/projects/darling-quarter/) describes the curved plan, vaulted atrium, timber mullions, adjustable louvres, public passage between the two buildings and roof linking different wing scales. Actual photographs opened and visually inspected: [park-facing curved facade and retail canopy](https://fjcstudio.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/03-scaled.jpg) and [timber/louvre facade detail](https://fjcstudio.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/04-5-scaled.jpg). The bank also publishes a [CBP South exterior image](https://www.commbank.com.au/content/dam/caas/newsroom/images/2017-06-15-newsroom-image-cbp-south.jpg) in its newsroom; it supports identity but was not needed as the visual construction reference.

[Horiso's contractor case study](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/5c103ae697d06ec3a08dd95d/5c2c179a4fdbba1140c5c471_Case_study_Darling_Quarter_Horiso_2012.pdf) describes the shaped glass atrium ceiling and its specialized shading. The model uses the mapped curved building outlines, nine facade bands, deep warm mullions, filtered horizontal louvre patterns, a park-facing timber awning and a subdivided curved roof. The vault profile, exact roof height and detailed window/shading positions are reconstructed rather than taken from an engineering plan. The real gap between North and South is preserved. No retail tenant, entry sign placement or interior fit-out is invented.

## Verification and integration

Run `./tools/runtime/godot --headless --path game --script ../source/bank_landmark_test.gd`. Native screenshots: run without `--headless` and append `-- --visual`; files are written to `/tmp/harbourlife-bank-review/`. Tests cover distinct building identities, geographic conversion, paired-campus relative position, nine OSM exclusions, identical structural mesh/collision faces, triangle winding, height boundaries, separate tower wings, curved roof solids, geographic physics rays, and damage/restore behavior. Repeated detailing is attached and batched by building component, so facade details disappear with their parent floor.

OSM attribution: © [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), ODbL. Source polygons are in `source/map-data/city.json`. `metadata()`, `footprints()` and `excluded_way_ids()` expose the integration data; `build(world)` constructs the bank models using existing world helpers. Small facade attachments are visual details while closed building solids provide collision. Exterior photographs remain reference-only URLs.

## Western Sussex mass integration correction

The exported candidate exposed a real context defect: the ordinary mapped `way/544300934` extrusion ran from ground to the inferred 63m top inside the Westpac podium. Its footprint contained both ends of the public colonnade route. A player starting inside this closed triangle shell could walk inside it without a wall contact, while the production save migration correctly detected occupied building volume. This was **not** a harmless capsule-versus-column approximation, and migration tolerances have not been relaxed. The failed candidate and dedicated probe remain in the ignored release/probe reports.

All five nodes of this mapped western outline are existing Westpac podium nodes. [Cushman & Wakefield's 2018–19 commercial sales review](https://www.cushmanwakefield.com/-/media/cw/apac/australia/insights/research/cw_comm_sales_booklet_18_19.pdf) identifies the western Sussex Tower as part of Westpac Place, with 21 levels, and the eastern Kent Tower as the taller 32-level portion. This corroborates the same-building identity; it does not establish the model's vertical dimensions. The previous description of way544300934 as a separate neighbour was incorrect and has been removed.

The conservative replacement uses that exact mapped outline and preserves the previous **63m estimated upper envelope**. The existing raised podium occupies 7.5–12m; the new Sussex solid starts at 12m and is subdivided into **17 three-metre damage bands**. These bands are gameplay/facade subdivisions, not a claim about measured storeys. The original column locations, public walk route, tower beacon and other 140 bank component IDs are unchanged. Nine exact OSM IDs now participate in generic exclusion; unrelated nearby buildings remain present. The namespace for the replacement is `bank/westpac/sussex/00` through `/16`. Historical damage keys for the retired erroneous `osm/way/544300934/storey_group/*` extrusion remain save history; they are **not** claimed to map one-to-one onto these new components.

Final local regression: **93/93 passed**, exit 0, zero `ERROR`, `WARNING` or `FAIL`, **140,005 triangles / 157 components**, an additional **544 triangles / 17 components** over the previous 139,461/140 public-detail pass. Logs: `reports/bank-v016-sussex-fix.log`; report: `reports/bank-v016-detail/checks.json`. The report includes model, test, CityMap and migration SHA256 values. It exercises the actual compiled map record through production `City._reserved`, `build_buildings` and migration geometry, verifies the original failed ground pose is safe, and retains closed-solid rejection inside the upper building. Destroy/restore removes and restores that upper occupancy. All three unchanged production-Player routes again pass, including the real Westpac walk endpoint's save-migration check. This is a targeted isolated fixture plus real mapped record, not a substitute for a final complete-App visual rerun.
