# Aviation models and handling, 0.1.3

The three aviation assets are original procedural geometry. Reference photographs were inspected for the silhouettes and construction details below. No photograph pixels, manufacturer CAD, or third-party model files are distributed. Names used for vehicle instances remain compatible with existing saves.

The helicopter's rotor has a complete swept-disc collision envelope, so safe placement accounts for every blade phase. Its Fenestron uses separate annular collision sectors, keeping the central opening open. Wheels and rotor assemblies animate independently. Static detail meshes are merged by material.

Manufacturer dimensions establish visual scale, not certified simulation performance. The requested game maxima are 420 km/h for the supercar, 320 km/h for the motorcycle, 800 km/h for the airliner, and 350 km/h for the helicopter. These are deliberately arcade handling targets. In particular, 350 km/h is not a claim about H160 certified performance; Airbus quotes recommended cruise of 255 km/h. These limits are reached through ordinary input, thrust/tyre forces and drag. Steering authority decreases with speed, and braking applies finite physical forces rather than changing displayed speed or overwriting velocity.

## Glide activation and saved motion

A newly summoned glider receives 28 m/s forward and 0.22 m/s descent; a paraglider receives 11.5 m/s forward and 0.52 m/s descent. The controller uses one shared trim-speed table for both the launch impulse and lift calculation. This avoids starting the paraglider below the airspeed required by its lift model and dropping before gravity accelerates it. Spawn planning reserves the complete wing envelope and three seconds of forward departure clearance. The glider is placed at least 180 m above sea level and the paraglider at least 120 m, or higher when needed to clear the player's surroundings. The normal aircraft entry path schedules this single launch impulse in the first live physics integration step. This prevents Jolt activation from replacing a velocity that was written while the vehicle was frozen.

An already moving, unfrozen wing is not relaunched when the player re-enters it. Pending launch velocity is included when saving before that first physics step; loading also restores linear and angular motion during native integration. There is no continuing engine force for either wing. Lift remains perpendicular to relative airflow and drag removes energy, so sustained glide trades altitude for travel.

## Verification

- `source/air_vehicle_model_test.gd`: geometry winding, finite positions, manufacturer scale, preserved wheel contact planes, animated assemblies, merged geometry budget, and helicopter swept-rotor collider. `-- --visual` records local native images for visual inspection.
- `source/air_vehicle_flight_test.gd`: production summon/entry, minimum launch height, first-second and first-three-second descent, 15-second glide without stalls, mechanical-energy decrease, immediate-save launch motion, ordinary reboarding, and saved-flight restoration. Default builds the complete world; `-- --local` uses the same controller with a lightweight test world. The tightened flight check requires less than 22 m height loss over 15 seconds and less than 2 m/s peak sink; this remains an unpowered glide, not a hovering mode.
- `game/scripts/air_vehicle_validation.gd`: the same validation runs inside exported applications through `--air-vehicle-qa`; it creates no persistent world save.
- `source/vehicle_speed_test.gd`: all four vehicles begin stationary on a test surface, accelerate solely through player input, then steer and brake at actual speed. This tests physics, not full-city performance or certified aerodynamics.

Final measured screenshots and regression logs are recorded by the release process after integration; source assertions alone are not a visual-quality acceptance.
