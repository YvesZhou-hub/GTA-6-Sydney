# Sydney named building references

Research and reconstruction: 10 September 2026. Implementation: `game/scripts/city_landmarks.gd`. These four buildings are native, metre-scale procedural meshes with structural collision and removable damage components. They are **photo-based exterior reconstructions**, not laser scans or a claim of a complete 1:1 Sydney. No reference photograph, third-party texture, raster logo asset or interior plan is redistributed.

## Location and evidence quality

The existing world projection is retained: `X=(longitude−151.2105)×92400`, `Z=(−33.86−latitude)×111320`; +X is east and +Z south. Ground is the game's 4.5m datum, not a site-specific AHD survey. Polygon centroids come from the OSM geometry, not street-address geocoding. All footprint vertices are converted to local metres and retained to 0.00001m numerical precision; that precision does **not** imply OSM has survey accuracy.

## International Tower One / HSBC

The current [HSBC Australia company page](https://www.about.hsbc.com.au/) identifies its headquarters in Barangaroo; [HSBC's contact directory](https://www.hsbc.com/contact-us) gives One International Towers Sydney, 100 Barangaroo Avenue. This is the headquarters' host building, not an invented standalone “HSBC tower”.

[RSHP's International Towers project](https://rshp.com/projects/office/international-towers-sydney/) and [completion account](https://rshp.com/news/archive/rshp-completes-international-towers-at-barangaroo-sydney/) describe the architect's tower family, cores, floorplates and external environmental treatment. [CTBUH's Tower One entry](https://www.skyscrapercenter.com/building/barangaroo-southinternational-towers/13280) supplies the 217m architectural height. Its historical address field is not preferred over the tenant's current official address.

The [real Tower One exterior photograph on Wikimedia Commons](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/24/International_Towers_Sydney_tower_1.jpg) was opened and visually inspected in the browser. Its rounded glass body, narrow red sunshade blades, dense vertical mullions, horizontal metal floor edges, dark intermediate village bands and northern service-core articulation inform the model. The exact concave/rounded OSM tower outline is preserved instead of replacing it with a cylinder or box. Repeated metal details are merged into each of the 49 removable floor components.

## Bank of China, 140 Sussex Street

The bank's [official 2019 relocation notice](https://www.bankofchina.com/au/en/bocinfo/bi1/201907/t20190710_15913196.html) moves its Australian head office to Ground Floor, 140 Sussex Street from 12 August 2019. [The current official Australian company page](https://www.bankofchina.com/au/en/aboutus/) confirms this address. The older 39–41 York Street headquarters and the unrelated Hong Kong Bank of China Tower must not be substituted.

[TTW's Bank of China project](https://www.ttwengineers.com/projects/bank-of-china) describes refurbishment of the existing 1988 building's 15 levels, including rooftop garden/pergolas and service work. The project page was available in search during research; direct navigation subsequently redirected to its project index. [Rickard Engineering's facade account](https://rickardengineering.com/the-use-of-very-small-undercut-concealed-anchors-on-grc-fc-building-facades/) identifies tiled GRC facade panels at 140 Sussex Street. [Climate Active's building record](https://www.climateactive.org.au/buy-climate-active/certified-members/140-sussex-street-sydney) independently confirms the building's Bank of China association.

The building photo in [AFR's 2016 purchase report](https://www.afr.com/property/bank-of-china-buys-new-hq-for-130m-20160905-gr9c5a) was opened and visually inspected. It shows pale tiled piers/spandrels, dark rectangular glazing, a shallow outward central street bay and a glazed ground level. This photograph predates the 2019 refurbishment, so the reconstruction does not assert that entrance signs or every current facade detail match. The game adds neither an old ING sign nor a fabricated bank logo. The roof additions express documented use, with approximate geometry rather than an invented surveyed plan.

## The Ribbon / W Sydney

[Marriott's W Sydney overview](https://www.marriott.com/en-us/hotels/sydwh-w-sydney/overview/) supplies the address, hotel identity and rooftop facilities. The [Marriott-issued opening announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/w-hotels-unveils-architectural-icon-in-australias-waterfront-metropolis-with-the-opening-of-w-sydney-301955782.html) identifies the opening and curving building design. [CTBUH's Ribbon entry](https://www.skyscrapercenter.com/building/the-ribbon-hotel-and-residences/14434) gives the 90m height and 25 storeys.

The completed exterior on [Karabiner's project page](https://karabiner.com.au/projects/the-ribbon), specifically [this photograph](https://www.karabiner.com.au/projects/the-ribbon/a889c8b37021bc4f395a9f40d104d073.jpeg), was opened and visually inspected. It shows the long rising, rounded silhouette, blue-gray glass grid and warm metal perimeter. The model has a 29-degree plan orientation; its long axis runs southwest–northeast and its taller rounded end is northeast. Cubic curves generate the upper outline, and closed geometry is clipped into 25 floor bands without visible gold lines at the damage seams. The mapped podium follows the actual irregular footprint. Surrounding Western Distributor flyovers belong to the road/elevation model and are not invented as part of the hotel. The original v0.1.1 exterior omitted signage; the v0.1.3 additions below replace that omission. No IMAX or restaurant interior is claimed.

## The Exchange / Haidilao Darling Square

The [architect's completed project page](https://kkaa.co.jp/en/project/the-exchange/) records 2019 completion, 6,680m² and 7F, shifted floor plates and overlapping curved Accoya timber strips. Its [completed-building photo by Martin Mischkulnig](https://kkaa.co.jp/img/2019/12/52794ec0791daa69174ff46b332832ef.jpg) was opened and visually inspected: pale irregular horizontal timber, circular glass drums, broad terrace overhangs and a visually open ground level. [WoodSolutions' project record](https://www.woodsolutions.com.au/case-studies/exchange) confirms the address, construction material and floorplate/terrace arrangement. [The City of Sydney's library announcement](https://news.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/articles/new-library-at-darling-square-to-take-flight-in-2019) confirms two library floors and the completed timber-screen concept.

The [NSW planning portal BCA assessment](https://majorprojects.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/prweb/PRRestService/mp/01/getContent?AttachRef=SSD-7021%2120190227T091632.991+GMT) states seven storeys and effective height 25.55m, based on top occupied floor RL28.00 minus ground RL2.45. This is not a certified roof height. The game's approximate 30m roof envelope allows the upper restaurant volume above that occupied floor. The [operator's store directory](https://www.superhiinternational.com/serve/storesearch) identifies Haidilao at Level 5, The Exchange, 1 Little Pier Street; the selected branch remains the explicit working assumption until the user specifies another branch.

The model uses seven independently removable offset circular bodies with broad pale slab edges, dense irregular 3D timber strips and fine screen supports. Offsets, timber spacing and glazing division follow the observed appearance, not an as-built shop drawing. Timber is batched per floor and material, not thousands of scene nodes. Timber screens and other small facade attachments are decorative geometry; the closed structural body supplies collision, and both disappear together on damage. No retail interior is implied.

## Data rights and checks

Run `./tools/runtime/godot --headless --path game --script ../source/city_landmark_test.gd`. Add `-- --visual` without `--headless` for native screenshots in `/tmp/harbourlife-city-review/`. The check covers coordinate conversion, correct OSM replacement IDs, material/geometry bounds, outward triangle winding, exact structural mesh/collider correspondence, actual physics ray hits at each geographic position, damage and restoration, and a bounded combined triangle count. Native exterior views are inspected separately because correct bounds alone do not establish a recognizable silhouette.


## v0.1.3 W Sydney detail revision

The [official Marriott gallery](https://www.marriott.com/en-us/hotels/sydwh-w-sydney/photos/) was checked again, and the official exterior and porte-cochere photographs were actually viewed through their public image links. They confirm a white W on the harbour-facing elevation near the high rounded end, a two-storey recessed entrance, black glazing frames, copper cladding and a red sculptural W inside. [Corlette, the actual signage designer](https://www.corlettedesign.com/w-hotel-sydney-signage-design-and-wayfinding/), explains the Waratah-inspired surface and shows the red sign beside the blue illuminated passage. Those photographs are references only and are not distributed with the game.

The hotel now has the white facade W, geometric red entrance W with 228 small raised petal elements, a recessed copper-lined arrival space and repeated illuminated passage frames. The recess is subtracted from the bottom two mapped podium slices, with a physical floor and an open door bay; it is not a sign in front of a solid wall. Entry dimensions and individual panel/petal placement are photo-based estimates. The short arrival foyer ends at a closed rear wall: it does not reproduce guestrooms, the complete escalator journey or the rooftop pool.

Upper elevations retain the existing mapped orientation, 90m height and curved silhouette. Added two-metre mullion spacing and paired perimeter bands give physical facade relief; the curved upper glazing and central roof strip use darker finishes so they no longer resemble a plain white roof. This is a procedural reconstruction, not photogrammetry or a surveyed architectural model.

`source/city_landmark_test.gd` adds actual standing-capsule samples through the entrance and a floor-support ray. The complete four-landmark fixture has 107 independently destructible structure components and 251,334 triangles after this revision; the actual final count is written to `reports/city-v013-geometry.log`. Native views include the harbour elevation, aerial profile and entrance. World context and neighbouring structures remain a separate integration check.

## 12 September 2026 — W Sydney rolled envelope and arrival relief

A fresh visual comparison used the completed building on [Multiplex's project page](https://www.multiplex.global/au/projects/the-ribbon/). Its [large end-elevation photograph](https://www.multiplex.global/media/05hbxbwt/banner_the_ribbon.jpg?width=3840&height=1640&v=1dad1d8a71a9810) was actually rendered and inspected in a Chrome browser screenshot, followed by the [harbour overview](https://www.multiplex.global/media/1ctnrupx/banner_ribbon-w-sydney-2023_08_28_mpx_ribbon-057.gif?width=650&v=1dad1debaef22b0). The former is a close view of the broad metal edge cheeks, nested U-shaped louvres rolling from the roof around the high end, dark spaces between returns, and the fine rectangular curtain wall. These features were missing from the earlier simple extrusion. Multiplex identifies the building's position between the two Western Distributor viaducts; existing road geometry is retained.

Nine continuous, successively narrower and higher metal return paths now follow the original curved envelope. Broad metal cheeks and face fascias frame the glass; a dark inner end surface gives the returns visible depth. Each floor owns its own clipped part of the new work. The trim's warm-neutral metal is based on this daylight photo; the actual finish can appear gold under warmer light. The glass shader now uses the same 2m spacing as the physical mullions, avoiding two slightly different facade grids.

[Corlette's completed signage page](https://www.corlettedesign.com/w-hotel-sydney-signage-design-and-wayfinding/) was also opened and its entrance photograph actually viewed, including [the image beside its Waratah description](https://www.corlettedesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/W-Sydney-Signage-Design-Sydney_3@2x-80.jpg). It shows the red W's bent petals, a blue stair passage, pale concrete wall and adjacent folded hexagonal copper wall. The procedural arrival now has 126 hexagonal cells with dark recessed centres, and 228 tapered bent petals instead of cuboids. Copper cells belong to the side/rear wall IDs; the lights and ceiling fixtures belong to the existing ceiling ID, using the correct centred-body transform. The W remains a geometric interpretation of the sign. The blue space remains the existing short foyer closure, **not a simulated complete staircase or hotel interior**.

All original 32 W structure IDs, exact OSM footprint/centre, yaw, original solid/collider shapes, 90m primary envelope and open entrance are retained. The number of returns, their dimensions (roughly 0.4m trim, 2m fascia), individual cell sizes and depths are visual estimates, not shop drawings. The photographs are viewed as references only; none are downloaded into the game or redistributed. `capture_views()` now supplies named `w-sydney-harbour`, `w-sydney-ribbon-end` and `w-sydney-arrival` world-space cameras for final native captures in the populated city.

The W-stage headless fixture passes **30/30 checks**, **107 components**, **267,252 triangles**, versus the initial 251,334. `reports/city-w-detail-geometry.log` records the run. New checks cover the complete legacy W ID set, finite nested return paths, transformed copper-wall placement and ownership, 25 additional standing-capsule samples across the entrance width, capture metadata, and damage/restore of roof-roll and ceiling owners. No GPU or populated-city run was performed by this subtask; final visual quality and neighbouring occlusion require the parent task's native capture.

## 12 September 2026 — International Tower One shading assembly

The [architect's RSHP project account](https://rshp.com/projects/office/international-towers-sydney/) was reread and its specifically captioned **Tower 1** fin close-up, [6120_N13403.jpg](https://rshp.com/assets/uploads/6120_N13403.jpg), was actually opened as a full browser image and visually inspected. It shows deep fins with a red upper panel, pale lower perforated panel, dark framing and metal fixing arms. This is not the neighbouring Tower 2 photograph shown elsewhere on the same page. RSHP documents the common 1.5m curtain-wall module, 3m fin centres and panels up to 1.8m deep and 3.7m long.

Tower One now uses 3m fin centres aligned with 1.5m glazing modules, 3.7m framed blades, distinct red upper faces and a lower procedural dot pattern representing perforated metal. Thin connector arms are batched with the metalwork. The lower pattern is an opaque visual approximation, not thousands of physical drilled holes. Blade depth varies from 0.85 to 1.60m according to facade orientation; that distribution and the selective fixing-arm representation are estimates. Existing north core treatment, floor structure, podium, 217m height, map centre and 52 Tower One damage IDs remain unchanged. All work is facade geometry above the podium and does not enter pedestrian paths. Public/private interior layout is not inferred from sibling-tower lobby photos.

`capture_views()` adds `tower-one-whole` and `tower-one-fins`. The headless W + Tower One stage passes **32/32 checks**, retains **107 total components** and has **303,852 triangles**. The combined budget is now 350,000 triangles to cover real panel/frame geometry rather than the former single-box fins; all repeated details remain merged by floor and material. The test log is `reports/city-tower-one-detail-geometry.log`. Final populated-city visual inspection remains with the integrating parent task.

## 12 September 2026 — Bank of China Sussex Street colonnade

The [City of Sydney's July 2018 Nimbus Architecture / GroupGSA heritage report](https://eplanning.cityofsydney.nsw.gov.au/Common/Integration/FileDownload.ashx?ext=PDF&filesize=4382913&id=%21%21Qm%2BqLNdHt8o3pCZrBQGOVTn6SHeZ0UsR6kh%2BSg%3D%3Dx06o7QX%2Bb5s%3D&modified=2018-07-12T04%3A29%3A10Z) was actually rendered in Chrome's PDF viewer. Pages **1, 16, 17, 22 and 23** were inspected: the cover and field photos establish the building identity; page 16 Figure 8b shows the existing Sussex Street colonnade, setback glass and transverse beams; pages 22–23 show the proposed street elevation and ground-floor work. These are explicitly **2018 field photos and proposal drawings**, not a newly surveyed 2026 completion record. The project documents glazing/paving updates at that frontage. The [bank's current official address](https://www.bankofchina.com/au/en/aboutus/) still identifies Ground Floor, 140 Sussex Street.

The existing `city/boc/lobby` component now contains an open street colonnade, physical floor, overhead slab and nine solid columns. Glass is recessed 3.15m from the mapped west edge, with slender mullions and transverse metal beams; 336 upper-floor window modules gain projecting sill noses, masonry returns and dark seals. The continuous glass grid is adjusted to this narrower street-bay rhythm. Column count, 5.2m spacing, 3.15m depth and individual frame dimensions are estimates based on the photographs and coarse mapped envelope. The 16 legacy BOC IDs, exact mapped centre, primary footprint and estimated 58m height remain unchanged; only the existing lobby's solid geometry is opened to reproduce the documented public colonnade.

The old [Record door supplier case](https://www.recorddoors.com.au/en/references/487/bank-of-china-sydney) still appears in search with its revolving/adjacent swing-door description but direct navigation now redirects to the general case index. TTW's earlier project URL similarly redirects. Those unavailable photographs were **not** treated as seen. No particular revolving-door placement, private banking interior, signage installation or full 2019 fitout was invented from those text snippets. The unrelated 138 Sussex Street heritage building and the former 39–41 York Street headquarters are not substituted. Source PDF pages and photographs are not bundled with the game.

`capture_views()` adds `bank-of-china-sussex` and `bank-of-china-colonnade`. `walk_routes()` supplies world-space `name`/`points` dictionaries for the bank's pavement-to-colonnade-to-pavement route and W's public arrival, compatible with the parent application's precinct runner. The local fixture clips **actual production OSM land triangles** around these two approaches rather than building the city or supplying a fake interior floor. All walking uses `harbor_player.gd` and ordinary forward input, with normal acceleration, gravity, collision and floor snap; it makes no mid-route teleports or jumps. Static checks cover 30 standing-capsule/floor samples, column collision, outside-pavement clearance and the original ID sets. Structural report output is `reports/city-detail/checks.json`, including every check, final counts, model/test SHA256 hashes and the four continuous out-and-back walk results. The final console log is `reports/city-boc-detail-geometry.log`; native rendering and neighbouring occlusion remain the parent task's final integration check.

The initial three-building stage passed **44/44 checks**, **324,908 named-building triangles**, **107 original components**. Its unchanged evidence is preserved in `reports/city-detail/sourcefull44.json` and `sourcefull44.log`. Both W arrival and BOC colonnade were walked continuously outward and back by the production player with **zero airborne frames**, after damage/restore. Additional checks cover both BOC street entry bays across 1.2m of lateral clearance and the real overhead slab. The return route exits through the open bay between columns, not through the final solid column. The Exchange and shared mesh helpers were byte-compared to the current repository baseline and remain unchanged.

The subsequent integration review reproduced a save-migration false positive at three W floor positions and three BOC colonnade positions: the real controller stood safely with its foot origin 8.94–9.92mm below the floor top, but the earlier AABB/landmark whitelist rejected all six poses. All six deliberately buried 6cm poses were correctly rejected. `reports/city-detail/migration-baseline.json` and `migration-baseline.log` preserve that failing baseline. The integrating task corrected the migration rule to require a registered, intact custom support, upward normal, tightly bounded foot-to-floor distance, the actual production capsule, and closed-mesh torso/head clearance.

The final fixture passes **56/56 checks**, with the same **324,908 triangles**, **107 components**, four continuous walks and zero airborne frames. The extra twelve checks preserve six actual settled poses and reject their six buried counterparts. `reports/city-detail/checks.json` and `sourcefull56.log` are the final full evidence; the JSON includes model, fixture and migration SHA256 hashes. `-- --migration-only` runs those twelve pose checks alone and writes `migration-checks.json`; this mode records that triangles and continuous walks were not measured and does not overwrite the complete report. Its corrected run is also preserved as `migration-fixed.log`.
